# Drafting Authentic Reviews & Testimonials

Use when the user asks you to help write a review, testimonial, or recommendation for a product/service (Trustpilot, G2, Capterra, app stores, a client or vendor reference, etc.).

## Core principle: authenticity above polish
A review written from the user's *real* details reads authentic; a generic one reads like marketing and undermines value. Before drafting, collect the user's genuine experience. Do not fabricate specifics they didn't give you.

## Workflow
1. **Confirm the target + intent.** Where is the review going (Trustpilot, play store, etc.)? What star rating / sentiment do they want? If they just said "5-star," note it and still collect real facts to back it up.
2. **Interview — ONE QUESTION AT A TIME.** Ask a single question, wait for the answer, log it, ask the next. Do NOT dump a multi-question form. See the question sequence below. Keep answers in a running notes list ("✅ Logged: ...") so the user can follow progress.
3. **Draft.** Once you have enough (even a few lines), write the review in their voice, honest to what they shared.
4. **Offer a shorter variant** too (reviews often prefer brevity).
5. **Tell them submission is on them.** Review forms sit behind login + reCAPTCHA; you can draft but the user must log in and paste it. State this clearly.

## Interview question sequence (run one at a time)
1. What are you using it for?
2. What made you try it (and did something hold you back before / what changed your mind)?
3. What's the #1 thing you genuinely like?
4. Any real hiccups you hit that ended up fine? (Authentic reviews include friction that got resolved.)
5. Roughly how long have you been using it?
6. Who would you recommend it to?
7. Desired tone + rough length (casual vs professional).

## Pitfalls
- **NEVER ask all questions at once.** The single strongest correction in this workflow: the user explicitly said *"One q at a time pls"* after a 7-question form was dumped. Interview interactively, one question per message.
- Don't pre-write a generic polished draft before interviewing — that's what the user is trying to avoid.
- Don't claim you can submit the review. You can't get past their OAuth/reCAPTCHA.
- Skip any question the user doesn't care about; a few solid answers are enough to draft.
