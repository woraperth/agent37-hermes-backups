# Investment-property screening reference

Use this as a conservative first-pass template for an Australian second property.

## Inputs
- Purchase price (confirm this is not the loan amount)
- Weekly rent and realistic occupied weeks (use 50 weeks for a first pass)
- Candidate LVRs: 50%, 60%, 70%
- Base and stress interest rates
- Actual strata levy, council/water, landlord insurance, management fee, maintenance allowance, and land-tax position
- Existing home-loan balance, offset balance, household income, and emergency buffer

## Core calculations
- Annual gross rent = weekly rent × 52
- Gross yield = annual gross rent ÷ purchase price
- Loan = purchase price × LVR
- Interest-only interest = loan × annual rate
- Conservative pre-tax cash flow = (weekly rent × occupied weeks) − non-finance costs − interest
- P&I cash flow is lower than interest-only screening because principal is also paid.

## Worked screening example (illustrative, not a quote)
For a $600,000 apartment renting at $600/week:
- Gross rent = $31,200/year
- Gross yield = 5.2%
- At 50 rental weeks, rent used for screening = $30,000
- At 6% interest: $300K loan costs $18K interest; $360K costs $21.6K; $420K costs $25.2K.
- If non-finance costs are $8K–$12K/year, approximate pre-tax interest-only cash flow is:
  - 50% LVR: +$4K to $0
  - 60% LVR: +$0.4K to −$3.6K
  - 70% LVR: −$3.2K to −$7.2K

These ranges are highly sensitive to strata, special levies, vacancy, and actual rate. They are not a borrowing-capacity or tax calculation.

## Communication rules
- Separate gross yield, pre-tax cash flow, after-tax result, and loan serviceability.
- Say what is excluded (principal, tax, depreciation, major repairs, purchase costs).
- For NSW purchases, verify current transfer duty from Revenue NSW rather than relying on stale calculators.
- Recommend a broker for serviceability and an accountant/tax adviser for negative gearing, depreciation, ownership structure, and future CGT.
