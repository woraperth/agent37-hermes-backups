# Property Development Research

## When to use

Use when a user asks when an apartment or master-planned property development will be completed, settled, handed over, or when they should prepare finance. This is a class-level research workflow, not a substitute for reviewing the user's contract or regulated financial advice.

## Core procedure

1. **Pin the identity.** Record the project name, developer, street/suburb, tower or stage if known, and whether the question concerns a private apartment, social/affordable housing, retail, or the whole precinct. Do not merge similarly named projects.
2. **Set the evidence date.** Check the current date/timezone before describing a timeline. Treat old marketing pages and listing portals as historical evidence unless corroborated by a current official update.
3. **Start with primary sources.** Check the developer's project/community-engagement portal, planning authority or government project page, and project partners such as community-housing providers. Look for literal terms: `Project Status`, `Under Construction`, `Construction Update`, `completion`, `settlement`, `handover`, `occupation certificate`, and `stage`.
4. **Collect dated updates.** Prefer the newest construction update available. If updates are PDFs hosted on SharePoint or another document store, extract the link from the official HTML and inspect the document text; do not rely on the visible page's file list alone.
5. **Cross-check independently.** Use a second reputable source for a reported completion date, then distinguish it from the primary-source status. If sources conflict, show the old and new dates and explain why the newer official source carries more weight.
6. **Separate milestones.** Report separately: a particular tower/stage, private-apartment completion or settlement, social/affordable-housing completion, and whole-precinct completion. Construction completion is not automatically settlement: settlement depends on the contract, occupation/registration, lender valuation, and developer notices.
7. **Answer at supported precision.** Use a year or broad period when that is all the evidence supports. Never invent a month from a year-only statement. Clearly label old estimates as stale or superseded.
8. **Translate into finance preparation.** Give a practical, non-personalised checklist: review the contract's estimated settlement and sunset dates; speak with a mortgage broker early; refresh borrowing capacity and documents 6-9 months before the expected settlement window; retain a buffer for valuation shortfall, deposit balance, stamp duty, legal fees, and body-corporate costs. Recommend confirming the actual tower/stage and settlement notice with the developer or conveyancer.
9. **Cite while drafting.** Register sources before writing, cite every externally sourced date/status/number inline, and finish with a generated Sources list. For financial planning, attach literal evidence quotes where feasible and state that lender policy and the signed contract control.

## Pitfalls

- Repeating an original off-the-plan estimate after a newer government or developer update supersedes it.
- Treating “the project” as one handover date when different towers or tenures have different milestones.
- Confusing construction completion, occupation certificate, registration, settlement, and move-in.
- Giving a precise month based only on a year-level source or a search snippet.
- Treating a listing portal's “estimated completion” as a contractual settlement date.
- Presenting borrowing-capacity guidance as approval or financial advice.
- Citing a search-result snippet as if the linked article had been read.

## Verification checklist

- [ ] Project/developer/location and stage are pinned.
- [ ] Current official status was checked.
- [ ] Latest dated update was considered.
- [ ] Conflicting historical dates are explicitly reconciled.
- [ ] Milestone type is named for every date.
- [ ] Contract and lender caveats are included.
- [ ] Inline citations and Sources list match the retrieved sources.
