# Social-post voice matching

Use this skill when a user asks for a post, thread, or article in the style of a public social-media sample, especially when the sample is on X/Twitter and the requested output is in Thai.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the linked post itself before drafting. Prefer browser extraction of the author-authored text and the complete thread. Search snippets can help locate a source but are not a reliable substitute for the post.
2. Separate voice from content. Extract concrete voice traits: opening move, paragraph length, numbering, punctuation, use of English terms in parentheses, degree of first-person framing, rhetorical questions, level of explanation, and closing style.
3. Verify the requested subject independently when it is a named framework, course, product, or concept. Use an official source when available. Distinguish what the public source explicitly says from what is only an inference.
4. Do not copy distinctive sentences, metaphors, or the structure so closely that the result becomes a disguised reproduction. Match the broad voice and rhetorical habits while writing original content.
5. Draft for the target channel. For an X-style Thai thread, favor a short personal setup, numbered sections, conversational explanations, occasional English terms with Thai clarification, and a practical conclusion. Keep paragraphs easy to scan on mobile.
6. Run an anti-AI pass. Remove generic praise, inflated significance, repetitive three-part lists, excessive headings, decorative emojis, and canned closers. Keep a human point of view and specific examples.
7. If a framework acronym is not fully documented, do not invent an expansion. Describe the verified framework at the concept level, or explicitly label an interpretation as an interpretation.
8. Present the finished draft directly. Mention source limitations only when they materially affect accuracy; do not burden a ready-to-post draft with unnecessary research notes.

## Thai social-post style checklist

- Start with why the writer encountered the idea and why it was worth sharing.
- Use natural Thai rather than formal translated prose.
- Keep numbered points focused on one idea each.
- Explain technical or English terms briefly when first introduced.
- Use examples that show how to apply the idea, not just definitions.
- End with a compact synthesis or a question the reader can use to test the idea.

## Pitfalls

- Do not treat an X page title or search-result snippet as the complete source text.
- Do not claim to know the private contents of a paid course from a public landing page.
- Do not silently turn a plausible acronym expansion into a sourced fact.
- Do not overfit to one sample's exact number of points; preserve the author's rhythm, not a rigid template.
